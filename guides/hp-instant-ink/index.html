How HP Instant Ink counts a page, and what happens to the cartridges when you cancel

By Which Ink Fits · Checked 20 Nov 2026

Any ink on the sheet counts as one page, and both sides count as two. If you cancel, HP's terms say the plan runs through the last day of the current month and HP then remotely disables the subscription cartridges. The three HP pages below do not describe the leftover ink in exactly the same sentence.

What HP counts

HP's Instant Ink terms, section 6, define a printed page in capital letters: a page "upon which any amount of printing is performed" by the printer, "including any photos." The next sentence says printing both sides of the same sheet counts as two pages. A page is the media type and size in the printer's specifications. HP's enrollment support page says the same thing in plainer language: color, black and white, and photo prints are counted the same way.

A plan is a number of those pages per month, not a number of cartridges. The FAQ page, read 20 Nov 2026, said plans started at $1.99 for 10 pages a month and went up to $32.99 for 700 pages a month, and that extra pages were sold in sets of 10 to 15 pages for $2, with the set size depending on the plan. Those prices are what that FAQ printed that day. The plan menu on hpinstantink.com is the list that can change. The same FAQ says the average family uses the 100-page plan and the average small business uses the 700-page plan. That is HP's description of its customers, not a measurement we made.

Rollover is capped, and a yearly plan is different

Unused pages on a monthly plan roll over. The terms cap the rollover you can carry into the next month at three times the monthly ink allotment, or two times for toner. Anything above that cap is forfeited. HP's FAQ uses the same ink cap and gives the example: a 50-page plan can roll 150 pages. The terms then say the total you can have at the start of a month is four times the ink allotment, or three times for toner: the new month's pages plus the capped rollover.

A yearly plan does not work that way. Footnote 2 on the FAQ says unused yearly-plan pages do not roll into the next year. The enrollment support page says the yearly plan was, at the time of that page, offered only in the United States, and that cancellation inside the first 30 days is immediate. After 30 days, that support page says cancellation waits until the end of the yearly cycle or until the plan pages are used up.

The cartridges are not yours to keep using

The terms say HP retains ownership of the subscription cartridges and supplies them only so you can use the service. They work with the printer named on the account. The terms say you may not move them to another printer, use them outside the service unless HP says you can, or sell them. HP's FAQ says the cartridges in the welcome kit only work with an Instant Ink subscription. Shipping is not every month: the printer reports ink levels, and HP ships when it decides you need cartridges.

The printer has to stay online. The terms say that if it is not connected to the internet, the subscription cartridges are disabled. HP's FAQ says the service needs Wi-Fi so HP can see the ink level. That is separate from cancellation. Unplugging the printer does not end the billing, and the terms say so.

What the three HP pages say about canceling

You can cancel online at hpinstantink.com or at the cancel link in the terms, or by calling HP. The terms say the cancellation takes effect on the last day of the current month period. Their example: a period that runs from 15 January to 15 February, with a cancel request on 30 January, ends on 15 February. The last month is charged in full. There is no refund for plan pages or rollover pages you did not print, and no refund for unused ink. Removing the cartridges, not printing, or leaving the printer offline does not cancel the plan.

On what is left in the cartridges, the pages diverge.

The terms, in the cancellation section and again in the cartridge section, say HP will remotely disable the subscription cartridges so they no longer print. The consequences paragraph also says you must stop using the service and return the cartridges. An earlier paragraph says HP requests that you return empty or cancelled cartridges for recycling, and lists cases in which you may return them. Both paragraphs are in the same terms document.

HP's FAQ says the Instant Ink cartridges will not work after the end of the billing cycle in which you cancel, and that you go back to HP original Standard or XL cartridges. Footnote 1 adds that, for a printer that is not HP+, HP's own words also allow third-party cartridges HP describes as compatible. Which Ink Fits does not recommend for or against other ink. The FAQ also says there is no cancellation fee, and that a downgrade or a cancellation applies after the last day of the current billing period.

HP's enrollment support page says you can keep printing with the Instant Ink supplies until the end of the current billing cycle, and that you then remove those cartridges and install regular ones. It says the final monthly charge comes at the end of the cycle, with an email when you cancel and another when the cycle ends.

One exception is easy to miss. If the cartridge that shipped in the printer box was converted into a subscription cartridge when you enrolled, the terms say that cancelling while that cartridge is not yet empty may let you revert to using it as an in-box cartridge. The terms tell you to read the papers that came with the printer if it shipped with subscription cartridges. That sentence is narrower than "the cartridges always keep working" and narrower than "they always die on the last day."

Toner, and which printers

HP's FAQ says Instant Ink for toner is closed to new customers on the LaserJet 100 and 200 series and the LaserJet 3000 and 4000 series. People already subscribed are not affected, according to that FAQ. A toner printer on this site, such as the LaserJet Pro M428, is still a toner number (58A), not an Instant Ink plan.

The same FAQ prints a long eligible-model list. Checked 20 Nov 2026, that list includes the DeskJet 2750e series, the ENVY 6050e series, and the OfficeJet Pro 9010 series. Our pages for the DeskJet 2755e, the ENVY 6055e, and the OfficeJet Pro 9015 are in those series. Eligibility is HP's list, not ours. The cartridge number on those pages is the one in HP's compatibility document, which is what you match if you are not printing on the subscription.

Frequently asked questions

What does HP count as one page?

HP's Instant Ink terms define a printed page as a page on which the printer puts any amount of ink, including a photo. Printing both sides of one sheet counts as two pages. Color, black, and photo pages count the same way on HP's enrollment support page.

Do unused pages carry over?

On a monthly plan, HP's terms say unused pages roll over, up to three times the monthly ink allotment (two times for toner). A 50-page ink plan can hold 150 rollover pages, which is the example on HP's FAQ. HP's FAQ says a yearly plan does not roll unused pages into the next year.

What happens to the cartridges if I cancel?

HP's terms say cancellation takes effect on the last day of the current month period, the last month is charged in full, and HP then remotely disables the subscription cartridges. HP's support page says you can keep printing with those cartridges until that day, then remove them and install regular cartridges. Taking the cartridges out, or unplugging the printer, does not itself cancel the plan.

Are Instant Ink cartridges the same as the numbers on the printer pages here?

No. The cartridge pages on this site repeat the part numbers in HP's compatibility document, such as 67 for the DeskJet 2755e. Instant Ink cartridges are subscription supplies. HP's terms say HP keeps ownership and that they work only in the enrolled printer.
